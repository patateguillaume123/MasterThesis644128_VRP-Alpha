Does the Firm-Level Variance Risk Premium Predict ShortVariance Returns? Evidence from U.S. Large-Cap Equities?

This thesis studies whether the firm-level variance risk premium (VRP) predicts the returns of
short-variance strategies in U.S. large-cap equities. The analysis uses a hand-picked panel of 73
prominent U.S. firms between 1996 and 2024, including ten firms that failed or were severely
distressed. The total VRP positively and significantly predicts the next-month short-variance
payoff (0.204, t = 5.60), although the R² is only 2.2% and the pooled slope is materially influenced
by the 2008-2009 crisis, whereas the Fama-MacBeth slope remains significant without it. Contrary
to the index-level evidence, the downside leg does not drive this predictability. A long-short
strategy selling variance in the highest-VRP quintile, and going long variance in the lowest, earns
an annualized alpha of 90.2% (t = 3.20) after controlling for the Fama-French five factors,
momentum and changes in the VIX, with alpha surviving plausible transaction costs and the
exclusion of the 2008-2009 crisis period. Overall, the results point to a firm-level premium that is
partly harvestable but concentrated in volatile periods and exposed to severe tail losses.
